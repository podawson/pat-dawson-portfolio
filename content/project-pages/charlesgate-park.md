## The public brief

### Repair began with the record.

Charlesgate was once a connector in Boston’s park system before a highway buried the Muddy River and split the landscape from its history. Rather than treating the residual space as blank, the project combined archival plans, point-cloud survey, automated tree modeling, public hearings, and maintenance planning. Daylighting the river became both ecological infrastructure and a way to make the site’s loss legible. The design translated that evidence into a phased sequence the city and community could evaluate and maintain.

## Who defines repair

Community stakeholders shaped the brief; city agencies retained authority over long-term maintenance and delivery.

## Details

### 01 · Recover the record

Historical plans and environmental research located the culverted river and made the site’s political history part of the design brief.

### 02 · Build the site model

Survey point clouds, a meshified highway, and automated tree placement turned an infrastructurally difficult site into geometry the team could reason with.

### 03 · Return it in phases

Public input, ecological repair, maintenance, and incremental access were coordinated as one delivery sequence rather than separate promises.
