## Operating story

Charlesgate was once a connector in Boston’s park system. Urban Renewal put a highway through it, buried the Muddy River, and left a public landscape cut off from both its ecology and its history. The project began by refusing to treat the residual space under the road as a blank site. Archival plans were used to recover what had been displaced; point-cloud survey became a workable mesh of the highway; Grasshopper automated tree modeling and placement across a large, irregular landscape. Those technical models sat beside public hearings, agency coordination, maintenance planning, and a phased approach to reuse. Daylighting the river was both ecological infrastructure and a way to make the site’s loss legible. The accountable parties stayed visible throughout: the public shaped the brief, agencies held the long-term maintenance burden, and the design team translated that record into a sequence that could actually be delivered.

## Where authorship stays human

The model organized evidence and geometry; community stakeholders and city agencies retained the final say over what repair meant and how it would be maintained.

## Details

### 01 · Recover the record

Historical plans and environmental research located the culverted river and made the site’s political history part of the design brief.

### 02 · Build the site model

Survey point clouds, a meshified highway, and automated tree placement turned an infrastructurally difficult site into geometry the team could reason with.

### 03 · Return it in phases

Public input, ecological repair, maintenance, and incremental access were coordinated as one delivery sequence rather than separate promises.
